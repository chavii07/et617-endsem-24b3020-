👟 Part A: The Three-Price Retail Story

Concept Anchor

Imagine you and your friend visit a sneaker shop in Pune. Notice the 3 distinct rupee numbers:

Step 1 • Factory Cost
CP: ₹1,000

Cost Price (CP)

What the shopkeeper paid the factory to bring the sneakers to the shelf.

Step 2 • Sticker Tag
MP: ₹1,500

Marked Price / MRP (MP)

The shiny printed sticker you see on the shoebox before any discount.

Step 3 • Cash Counter
SP: ₹1,350

Selling Price (SP)

What you actually hand over at the cash counter after 10% off the sticker!

⭐ The Two Golden Rules (Sharvee's Memory Anchor)
  • Rule 1: Discount is ALWAYS taken off the Marked Price (MP) — because customers only see the sticker tag on the box!
  • Rule 2: Profit or Loss is ALWAYS measured from the Cost Price (CP) — because the shopkeeper measures gain from their original factory investment!

Think about who looks at which number! When Sharvee walks into the shop, she looks at the printed tag sticker (₹500). That is the base for her discount.
Task 2: Step Workspace • School Bag Monsoon Sale
A school bag costs a shopkeeper ₹800 (Cost Price, CP). She puts an MRP tag of ₹1,000 (Marked Price, MP) and offers a 15% monsoon discount.
  • (a) Calculate the Selling Price (SP).
  • (b) Calculate her actual Profit % on CP.
  • (c) In 1–2 sentences, explain why you subtracted the 15% from ₹1,000 instead of ₹800.
Hint: 15% of ₹1,000 = ₹150 off the tag.
Hint: Compare rupee profit against the ₹800 factory cost.
Step 1: 15% of ₹1,000 is ₹150. Subtract ₹150 from ₹1,000 to get SP = ₹850. Step 2: Profit is ₹850 - ₹800 = ₹50. Step 3: Divide ₹50 by ₹800 and multiply by 100!
Task 3: Error Critique • Phoenix Mall Supermarket
At the supermarket, a ₹500 box of chocolates has a festive banner: "10% OFF + Extra 10% OFF at Checkout!".
Your friend Kabir claims: "10% + 10% means a flat 20% discount! So 20% of ₹500 is ₹100, and I will pay ₹400."
  • Is Kabir Right or Wrong?
  • What is the price after applying the first 10% discount?
  • What is the true final Selling Price after the second 10%?
  • Explain why 10% + 10% does NOT equal a flat 20% discount.
Remember: When the cashier applies the second discount, the chocolate box price has already dropped from ₹500 to ₹450. What is 10% of ₹450?
Task 4: What-If Prediction • Electronics Bazar Pune
A retailer purchases Bluetooth headphones at CP = ₹1,200 and prints an MRP sticker of MP = ₹1,600. During a festive sale, the owner demands: "We must earn at least a 10% profit on our Cost Price!"
  • What is the highest discount percentage on the Marked Price (MP) the retailer can offer?
  • Explain your strategy step by step.
Hint: First calculate minimum SP needed (1200 + 10% of 1200 = ₹1,320), then find the discount from ₹1,600.
10% profit on ₹1,200 is ₹120, so minimum SP is ₹1,320. The gap between MP (₹1,600) and ₹1,320 is ₹280. Remember to divide ₹280 by ₹1,600 (not ₹1,200)!
Task 5: Adaptive Micro-Scenario • Deccan Sports Mart
A sports shopkeeper buys a cricket bat for CP = ₹900 and tags it with an MRP of ₹1,200. During the cricket season, he offers a 15% discount.
  • (a) Find the actual Selling Price (SP).
  • (b) Calculate the shopkeeper's Profit % on CP.
  • (c) Explain which price you used to calculate the discount and why.
Remember the two golden rules: Discount is off the Marked Price (MRP tag), and Profit % is measured from the Cost Price (factory cost)!

Mental Math Mini-Booster: Quick 10% & 5% Trick

Never memorize long division! Use the one-zero shifting trick:

Step 1: 10% of Any Price

Just drop the last zero or shift decimal one place left!
10% of ₹500 = ₹50

Step 2: 5% is Half of 10%

Half of ₹50 is ₹25!
5% of ₹500 = ₹25

Step 3: 15% is 10% + 5%

₹50 + ₹25 = ₹75!
Quick & effortless!
